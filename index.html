About the programme

Rooted in Vedic tradition, held gently for today

Mission — to bring the sacred discipline of Garbh Sanskar to expecting families, in a form that fits real pregnancies and real weeks.

Philosophy — the womb is the child's first classroom. What a mother hears, eats, feels and is surrounded by shapes the being growing within her — so we work with sound, food, breath and relationship together, not in isolation.

Values — reverence for tradition, evidence-aware wellness, and the belief that a pregnancy is carried by two people, not one.

How the programme unfolds

Your nine months, held in three parts

Garbh Sanskar is paced with the pregnancy itself. Each trimester has its own focus, so the practices always meet you where you are.

Trimester One · Months 1–3

Rooting the light

Establishing the intention (sankalpa) and settling the body.

  • Sankalpa & mantra initiation
  • Foundational Ayurvedic diet reset
  • Rest, gentle breathwork, easing early symptoms
Trimester Two · Months 4–6

Nurturing the glow

The steadiest phase — deepening daily practice and bringing the partner in.

  • Daily meditation & prenatal yoga
  • Music, story and positive-reading sessions
  • Couple bonding rituals begin
Trimester Three · Months 7–9

Welcoming the flame

Preparing body, mind and family for the arrival.

  • Birth-preparation visualisation & breathing
  • Godh Bharai–style blessing ritual
  • Postpartum & early parenting orientation

Before you join

Common questions

Which trimester can I join from?

Any point in pregnancy. If you join mid-way, your first session includes a short catch-up so you're aligned with your batch's current trimester module.

Do husbands or partners need to attend every class?

Partners are welcome at every session and specifically invited to the couple-bonding rituals. Attendance for other classes is flexible.

Is this suitable for a high-risk or medically supervised pregnancy?

Our sessions complement, but never replace, your doctor's care. Please continue all medical advice and check with your obstetrician before starting any new practice, especially yoga or dietary changes.

What languages are classes conducted in?

Sessions are held in English and Kannada, with Hindi on request for online batches.

What do I need for an online class?

A quiet corner, a mat or cushion for seated practice, and a stable internet connection. A notebook for reflections is optional but encouraged.
